HOW OFFERGRID WORKS

Transparent price intelligence.

OfferGrid is a price-intelligence purchasing assistant designed to show the evidence behind a purchasing decision: verified current prices, recorded history, same-product market comparisons, match confidence, and clearly disclosed ranking factors.

01

We record prices we actually observe.

Historical charts begin when OfferGrid starts tracking a product. We do not invent prices from before that point.

02

We separate deal quality from seller claims.

Deal Heat uses recorded price history, markdown depth, offer quality, and whether a price is a new recorded low.

03

We show when our confidence is limited.

Exact identifiers are preferred. Without them, OfferGrid refuses to combine listings that conflict on model, product family, pack count, capacity, weight, size or power.

TRACKED COVERAGE

What “lowest” and “best” mean on OfferGrid

OfferGrid compares the offers currently discovered, tracked, and eligible for the applicable comparison. Unless a label expressly says otherwise, “lowest,” “best,” “market low,” and similar terms are dataset-scoped descriptions, not claims that OfferGrid has searched every merchant or purchasing option everywhere. A lower price can exist outside the sources or offers currently known to OfferGrid.

Shipping, tax, membership pricing, personalized promotions, coupons, rebates, local pricing, bundles, financing, trade-ins, and other checkout-specific terms may not be available in source data. The merchant's checkout page remains the final source for the transaction amount and terms.

DATA ACCURACY

How OfferGrid verifies eBay pricing

eBay search results are used to discover listings efficiently, but OfferGrid treats the exact eBay item endpoint as the higher-confidence source for public price claims. Published eBay offers enter a separate detail-verification queue, and a product page can refresh a stale display offer before showing it.

OfferGrid keeps stock quantity, lot size, title pack count, per-buyer quantity limits, unit pricing and base listing price as separate fields. A seller having many units in stock does not make an item a multi-pack, while a real multi-pack remains a distinct product variant.

The item price is a base item price. Shipping, tax, coupons, quantity discounts and checkout-specific promotions can change the final amount. OfferGrid does not invent a universal checkout total when the source API cannot prove one.

Market comparisons use only offers whose product relationship is supported by a strong canonical identifier. Title similarity by itself is never enough to make a same-product comparison claim. Weak-identity products can still show their own source listing and real price history, but cross-store comparison is withheld until identity can be proven. The displayed comparison currently uses item price. Shipping, tax, membership pricing, coupons, item condition, and bundle contents can change the final checkout total.

Search results are candidates, not proof. Every secondary eBay listing must pass exact item-detail identity verification before it can influence seller comparisons, top tracked-offer selection, Deal Heat, or price history. If the product has no provable GTIN, Brand+MPN, or ePID+Brand identity, OfferGrid withholds exact competitor comparison rather than guessing from similar titles. Fixed-price listings are used for competitor searches. When product condition is known, OfferGrid avoids mixing broad condition groups such as new and used. Seller tables rank the tracked item price; shipping, tax, coupons, and checkout-specific discounts remain separate and should be verified before purchase.

AUTONOMOUS CATALOG GROWTH

How OfferGrid discovers new products

The discovery scheduler wakes every 20 minutes and rotates through a bounded subset of enabled searches. This keeps product discovery continuous without rerunning every search on every cycle.

If an eBay search completes without creating a new normalized product, OfferGrid can deterministically synthesize additional searches from a category-specific product-family vocabulary. The expansion process is rule-based rather than generative AI: the same query and catalog state produce the same related phrases.

Shopper-generated searches use a stricter path: OfferGrid first measures how many catalog products actually satisfy the query intent. If the requested page cannot be filled, it searches connected marketplaces for additional exact matches, verifies them, adds qualifying products to tracking, and repeats within a bounded search budget. It never pads a page with merely adjacent category items; when exact results are exhausted, the page ends explicitly.

User-generated discovery rules remain tied to the original query. When one literal marketplace request is too thin, OfferGrid may add conservative context while preserving every meaningful original term; returned candidates still have to pass the original query before tracking or publication. It never falls back to unrelated category suggestions. Explicit sexual searches and explicit products are rejected before marketplace discovery or catalog persistence.

BUSINESS MODEL

How the purchasing assistant is funded

OfferGrid is built as a price-intelligence product, not a merchant. Some outbound merchant links are affiliate links, which means OfferGrid may receive a commission after a qualifying purchase. Squirrel or other affiliate technology may also monetize eligible merchant links. This does not change the recorded historical observations or exact listing identity, and the relationship is disclosed so visitors can account for it when evaluating recommendations.
